Initial Feasibility of Medical Billing

A practical operating model for a New Jersey client-facing company and a Pakistan delivery company—designed around clear ownership, verified compliance, controlled access to PHI and client-led scaling.

Executive recommendation

Proceed through a formal contractual collaboration

The NJ company owns the US commercial relationship. The Pakistan company owns delivery staffing and execution. Neither entity should blur bank accounts, contracts, data responsibilities or authority.

Start focused

Secure one to three independently owned outpatient practices in a specialty the operations lead understands. Physical therapy, behavioural health and rehabilitation are practical starting points.

Avoid at launch

Do not begin with patient debt collection, hospital contracts, DME, complex anesthesia, large fixed premises or unsupported coding decisions.

ALL REGULATORY INFORMATION IS RESEARCH-BASED. ACTUAL REQUIREMENTS AND LICENCE FEES MUST BE VERIFIED WITH OFFICIAL AUTHORITIES AND QUALIFIED ADVISERS BEFORE COMMITMENT.
01
Operating model

How the collaboration works

A clean handoff between US commercial control and Pakistan delivery execution keeps accountability visible.

New Jersey company

  • Own or license the US-facing brand, website, CRM and client contracts.
  • Market to NJ healthcare providers and qualify prospects.
  • Issue proposals, sign the MSA, SOW and provider BAA.
  • Invoice providers for service fees without holding payer or patient funds.
  • Manage relationships, escalation, insurance, tax and regulatory correspondence.
  • Approve delivery quality, reports and corrective action.

Pakistan company

  • Recruit, train and supervise the RCM workforce.
  • Perform eligibility, charge entry, claim submission and payment posting.
  • Manage rejections, denials, AR follow-up and approved appeals.
  • Support prior authorization and credentialing when included in the SOW.
  • Maintain QA, confidentiality, endpoint security and access logs.
  • Report incidents immediately and return or destroy client data at termination.
Activities the Pakistan company must not perform
It must not independently alter clinical documentation, invent codes, upcode, make unsupported medical-necessity decisions, promise payment outcomes or subcontract PHI work without written approval.
02
Governance

Contract and control structure

The minimum document stack creates a clear chain of authority, data responsibility and service accountability.

Client-facing documents

  • Provider Master Services Agreement
  • Provider Business Associate Agreement
  • Statement of Work and pricing schedule
  • Offshore-access disclosure and approval
  • Service-level agreement

Delivery and internal documents

  • Master Subcontracting Agreement
  • Subcontractor Business Associate Agreement
  • Data-security and incident-response schedule
  • Employee confidentiality, IP and acceptable-use agreements
  • Founder agreement covering commission, authority and client ownership

Company-controlled domains, CRM, bank accounts, password management and cloud tenancy are essential. Significant transfers should require two authorised approvals.

Shared completion rules
  • Assign one named owner from each team and one final approver to every workstream.
  • Record approvals, evidence, exceptions and dependencies; verbal agreement is not completion.
  • Do not access live PHI before contracts, BAAs, approved systems, access controls and workforce training are complete.
  • Do not accept new clients or material volume increases until Pakistan confirms capacity and NJ approves the commitment.
  • Review open risks and overdue actions jointly each week until the initial clients are stable.

06
Scalable delivery

Pakistan operational build

The operation should be sized from signed client workload—not from a speculative fixed-seat office.

Input

Signed client scope

Specialty, claim volume, services, systems, operating hours and compliance requirements.

Design

Capacity plan

Staffing, equipment, premises, software, security controls and supervision.

Approval

Verified operating plan

Official requirements, licence information, supplier quotations and documented workload.

Operational scope by maturity

Phase 1

Eligibility, demographics, approved charge entry, claim submission, rejection management, payment posting, denial follow-up, basic AR reports and QA.

Phase 2

Appeals, prior authorization, credentialing support, specialty reporting and provider dashboards.

Phase 3

Coding audits, patient financial support, advanced analytics and multi-specialty operations—only after legal, insurance and competency review.

07
Market entry

NJ client acquisition sequence

Prioritise independently owned outpatient practices with manageable workflows and a clear payer mix.

Ranked launch segments

  1. Physical therapy practices with 2–10 clinicians
  2. Behavioural-health and mental-health groups
  3. Chiropractic or rehabilitation clinics
  4. Family or internal medicine practices
  5. Dental practices when the team has the competence

Client development actions

  • Build a county-by-county prospect list.
  • Use local events and warm professional introductions.
  • Offer PHI-free workflow and denial discovery.
  • Demonstrate a synthetic KPI dashboard.
  • Be transparent about offshore delivery.
  • Provide one local point of accountability.
Avoid hospitals, health systems, DME, ambulance, anesthesia, large multi-state groups and complex Medicare or Medicaid portfolios until controls, references and specialist staff are proven.
08
Demand generation

Marketing after initial client validation

First prove the service with one to three clients. Build the wider marketing strategy from actual performance.

Before operations begin

Use founder-led outreach, local relationships, referrals and direct discussions. Do not commit to a fixed paid-marketing programme before the initial delivery model is validated.

After measurable delivery

Design the strategy using actual conversion, acquisition cost, specialty performance, team capacity, testimonials and payback. Expand only when the delivery operation can absorb new work.

09
Illustrative commercial model

Pricing and break-even logic

These examples describe potential client pricing and revenue—not setup or planning costs.

Illustrative startup pricing

5% of collected insurance revenue, subject to a minimum monthly service fee per practice, plus a scoped onboarding fee. A fixed-fee AR clean-up can be offered when the worklist is measurable.

The NJ representative should normally earn commission on attributable collected service revenue instead of receiving immediate company control. Equity should vest only against documented capital, qualified introductions and continuing responsibilities.
